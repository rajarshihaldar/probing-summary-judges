Evaluation Criteria:
Consistency (1-5) - the factual alignment between the summary and the
summarized source. A factually consistent summary contains only statements
that are entailed by the source document. Annotators were also asked to
penalize summaries that contained hallucinated facts.

Rubric:
- 5: All claims fully supported by the article. No hallucinations or
     distortions of any kind.
- 4: Nearly all claims supported. At most one minor imprecision that does
     not constitute a factual error.
- 3: Most claims supported, but one clear factual distortion or unsupported
     claim is present.
- 2: Multiple unsupported or distorted claims present, undermining factual
     reliability.
- 1: Pervasive hallucinations or claims that directly contradict the article.

Evaluation Steps:
1. Read the news article carefully and identify the main facts and details
   it presents.
2. Read the summary and compare it to the article. Check if the summary
   contains any factual errors or hallucinated facts not supported by the
   article.
3. Assign a score for consistency based on the Evaluation Criteria and
   Rubric above.
